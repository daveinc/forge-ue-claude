# Project inception contract

## Interview output

Produce:

- one-paragraph mandate;
- audience, platforms, input, camera, and performance target;
- core loop, fail/win conditions, progression, and session shape;
- narrative/tone/content boundaries;
- visual pillars, negative references, and storyboard/beat-board brief;
- scope boundaries, risks, hypotheses, and spike list;
- human decision owners;
- compact GDD sections with stable IDs.

Map gaps first, then ask one highest-value question at a time. Ask only questions whose answers can change scope, architecture, acceptance, or visual direction. Offer concrete options when useful, but allow the user to reject the framing. Record deferrable answers instead of turning every uncertainty into a blocker.

Before locking the mandate, generate at least three materially different interpretations when the design space is still open. Compare their player promise, production risk, technical consequences, art burden and testability. Keep the rejected tradeoffs in the decision ledger.

## Department launch rule

Launch the playable DAG when the core-loop contract exists. Launch visual, narrative, audio, research and QA workstreams when their minimum shared contracts exist. Do not wait for final art. Before any workstream consumes a shared asset, define its interface and invalidation edges.

Workers receive their objective, relevant GDD IDs, inputs, write scope, capabilities, acceptance criteria, evidence requirements, and referrals. They do not receive the entire interview or studio history.

The resident host creates the baseline and owns synthesis. Local workers receive decomposed packets only after their task and complexity class passes evaluation. Use them for parallel alternatives and heavy bounded source processing, not as automatic owners of a department.
